A complete DIY review workflow

This is not a list of generic dispute reasons. It is a practical workbook for building a record another person can follow.

1

Three-report setup

Obtain and preserve current reports, record source context, and build a bureau-by-bureau inventory.

2

Fact discipline

Distinguish a source fact, a difference, an unknown, and an assumption before making a claim.

3

Evidence packet

Index the records that matter, redact safely, and connect each enclosure to a specific factual statement.

4

Issue selection

Choose only truthful, supportable issues and match every request to what your records could establish.

5

Letter framework

Use exact letter anatomy and a fill-in framework without copying allegations that do not match your file.

6

Response decisions

Track delivery and timing carefully, compare responses to fresh reports, and document the reason for your next step.

Built to be used, not skimmed

Includes source registry, inventory, fact ledger, evidence index, issue sheet, drafting canvas, mailing log, response analysis, and master checklist.

The operating rhythm

Read. Document. Write. Track.

Every claim stays connected to a report page and a reliable record. Differences become review flags - never automatic proof.

Read

Review each bureau separately and use the report's own labels and legends.

Document

Preserve complete sources, record page references, and label unknowns honestly.

Write

Identify the exact field, exact issue, factual support, and evidence-matched request.

Track

Retain the sent packet, delivery record, response, fresh report, and next-step rationale.

Educational, not legal advice. The guide does not promise deletions, score changes, or any other result. It teaches a careful recordkeeping process and tells readers to verify current official instructions and rules for their facts.
